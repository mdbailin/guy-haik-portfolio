Ausangate Seven Lakes, October 9, 2017

October 9, 2017Guy Haik

On October 9 I spent a full day among the glacier lakes beyond Cusco, on the route sold as the Seven Lakes of Ausangate. I did not write down the name of the route at the time. Google Photos places my pictures next to a map marker with that name, and the day matches what the sources below describe: a day hike through high valleys and coloured lakes, with a hot spring as an optional ending. The weather was clear and sunny in all my photos, and almost every picture is of water and snow.

The start

My first photo is at 08:16, next to a small group of buildings with horses standing in front of them, a stream beside the path and a big snow peak behind. A photo at 14:20 shows the same buildings and the same horses, so I started and finished the day here. I did not note what the buildings are.

Up the valley

From 09:23 to 10:57 the photos follow a wide, open valley. There is a village of low houses with a terraced hill behind it, a dirt path with a stream running beside it, and riders on horses on the path ahead. At 10:23 two alpacas were on the stony slope next to the path, and at 10:57 a glacier-covered peak stood above a field with a stone hut at its edge.

The first lake and the pink shore

At about 11:06 I reached the first lake. The water is pale green-grey, and on one side the shore is pink sand. My photos here run from about 11:06 to 11:47. Someone had drawn a heart in the sand, and I did not note who. The snow peaks behind the lake are the best thing here, and I am not going to name them because I did not note the names.

The turquoise lakes

At 11:51 the colour changes. A lake of a deep turquoise lies below a dark rock crag, and the others I photographed until 12:44 are blue, turquoise or dark blue. At 12:02 there is a larger lake with a line of grass in the middle of it. Around 12:25 I sat beside a very dark blue pond, with my gloves on the rock next to me. At 12:40 I was in a marsh of grass tufts, with a pale lake behind it. I did not note how far we went, or which of these lakes was the highest point.

Back down

The next photo after the lakes is at 13:26, looking down a wide empty valley. At 13:40 there is a marsh with two white streams of water running down the slope above it. By 14:20 I was back at the buildings with the horses, with the same snow peak behind them.

The hot spring

The last picture of the day is in a hot-spring pool, with the snow peak and a row of low buildings behind it. It reached my gallery as a screenshot, so I do not have the original file or the exact time, only that it was taken that afternoon. The sources describe hot springs in the village of Pacchanta as an optional ending to this hike, but I did not note the name of the one I used, so I cannot say it was that one.

The route and the numbers today (checked November 2026)

This is research, not my experience. A Cusco tour company's guide to the route, published on June 20, 2026, gives the drive from Cusco to the start village of Pacchanta as about 3.5 hours, and the hike as 5 to 6 hours and about 16 km, with the main viewpoint at almost 4,800 m. It lists a protected-area entrance of about US$5, an optional hot spring at about US$6, and horses for rent for the climb to the viewpoint.

A second tour operator's brochure, which I could not find a date on, gives the same distance and time, a start at 4,100 m and a high point of 4,800 m. It says the entrance ticket is included in its tour and the hot spring is not, at 15 soles (about US$5).

The two pages differ slightly on the hot spring price, so check the current price on the day.

Season and altitude (checked November 2026)

An adventure-travel company's blog, dated September 16, 2026, calls May to September the best season, with clearer views and less rain, and April and October shoulder months with fewer crowds and mixed weather. The tour company above says October to April can bring rain, clouds and low temperatures. My day, October 9, was clear and sunny from morning to afternoon, but one clear day does not say anything about the next one.

Both sources advise spending at least two or three days in Cusco, or the Sacred Valley, before the hike, because the route is long and high. The tour company's page adds that the area is cold and windy, and recommends layers, a hat, a rain poncho and cash in soles.

A note on horses

On this route, as on quite a few other treks in South America, horses are offered to tourists so that they do not have to walk. I am strongly against using animals for that, and I do not recommend it. The sources above mention horses for hire only as information about what is on offer. If you are worried about the altitude, spend more days in Cusco first, and walk slowly.

What I would tell a friend

  • Start very early. My first photo is at 08:16 at the start village, and the last lake is at 12:44. The turquoise colours were strongest between 11:50 and 12:45, with the sun high.
  • Dress for cold and sun at once. I wore an alpaca sweater and sunglasses, and my gloves are lying on the rock in one of the photos.
  • This is a day of walking through high valleys, and the lakes are the reward. Do not expect a single big landmark like Machu Picchu.
  • Walk it yourself. Horses are offered at the start, and I am against using animals so that tourists do not have to walk (see the note above).
  • Bring cash in soles. The sources list the hot spring as a separate payment, and one of them lists the entrance separately too.
