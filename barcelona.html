Barcelona, December 2016

December 2–5, 2016 Guy Haik

Friday, December 2: arrival

We flew out of Ben Gurion in the morning and landed in Barcelona around midday. From the window, the snow on the Pyrenees was still visible on the horizon.

In the late afternoon we had seafood paella. It arrived in the pan it was cooked in, with a large prawn, mussels and clams on top, and olives on the side.

In the evening we walked through the Christmas market near the cathedral. The stalls sell figurines for the nativity scenes that Catalans set up at home, and the lights strung over the square give it the feel of a village fair.

From there we made our way down La Rambla to the Columbus Monument, lit against the night sky, and on to the harbour.

We stopped for a while on a terrace at Maremagnum, overlooking the harbour and the ferries lit up across the water. Dinner that night was pizza.

Saturday, December 3: Sagrada Família, Montjuïc, El Clásico

Sagrada Família

Saturday began at the Sagrada Família. I don't remember going inside, so what we saw was the building from the street.

Someone told us it would never be finished. I'm not sure that's true, but construction began in 1882 and the entrance fees of millions of visitors help pay for it. When I was there, the cranes were part of the view. Since then, the central Tower of Jesus Christ has been completed (February 2026), and the latest forecast for finishing the structures is around 2034.

Parc de la Ciutadella

In the Parc de la Ciutadella we found a large statue of a mammoth, with a few people stopping to take photos next to it. In the park, a street musician was playing the berimbau, the single-string instrument used in Brazilian capoeira.

El Born and lunch

We walked past El Born CCM, the old iron-and-glass market hall that is now a cultural centre.

For lunch we stopped at what I believe was Granja Xador, a traditional dairy shop. A note on our table had the name written on it, though I can't be completely sure after so many years. The original business has since moved, and the old address is now a different shop.

Montjuïc

We went up to Montjuïc in the early afternoon. I don't remember how we got there or what it cost. The photos I took on the way were shot from a moving vehicle, so we probably went up by road, past the Palau Nacional, home of the National Art Museum of Catalonia, and the four columns in front of it. We only saw it through the window.

From the viewpoint at the top, the whole port spread out below us: the harbour, the cable car towers and the city stretching along the coast.

Getting there today

Bus 150 runs from Avinguda de la Reina Maria Cristina up to Montjuïc Castle, about 20 minutes from Plaça d'Espanya. The funicular starts at the Paral·lel metro station (lines 2 and 3), and from its top station the castle is a steep 20 to 25 minute walk. The Montjuïc cable car runs from Avinguda Miramar to the castle. In 2026 the official site lists one-way adult tickets at €12 and round trips at €17.10, and in November and December it runs 10:00 to 18:00. Check the official site before you go, as service can be affected by strikes and by the season.

El Clásico at Camp Nou

This was the first football match I had ever been to, and it was a Clásico: Barcelona against Real Madrid at the Camp Nou. The stadium holds close to 100,000 people, roughly the population of a city like Givatayim, and it felt like it.

Before the match, the stands were still almost empty. By the time it was over, you could hardly move.

Before the match, the players warmed up on the pitch while the stands slowly filled.

As the teams lined up, the crowd held up red and blue cards and turned the stands into a giant mosaic.

Early in the match, Cristiano Ronaldo, number 7, was on the pitch for Real Madrid. Later, I filmed Lionel Messi, number 10.

Luis Suárez put Barcelona ahead in the 53rd minute, heading in a free kick from Neymar. The screen shows Barcelona 1, Real Madrid 0, with Suárez's goal in the 53rd minute under the score. Sergio Ramos equalised for Real Madrid in the 90th minute, again from a free kick. It ended 1–1. Afterwards, there was no room to move on the way out of the stadium.

Getting to the stadium today

The stadium is now called Spotify Camp Nou, and building work on it (Espai Barça) is still going on. By metro: line 3 to Palau Reial, Maria Cristina, Les Corts or Zona Universitària; line 5 to Collblanc, Badal or Ernest Lluch; also lines 9S and 10 to Collblanc. Trams T1, T2 and T3 also stop nearby. Tour and museum hours vary by season and events, so check the club's site.

The Magic Fountain

In the evening we went to the Magic Fountain at the foot of Montjuïc. The jets of water rise and change colour in a choreographed show with music, with a crowd gathered in front of it.

The Magic Fountain today

The fountain is on Plaça de Carles Buïgas, in the Poble-sec area below Montjuïc. Show days and times change with the season and have been suspended at times, so check the city's official site for the current schedule before you go.

Sunday, December 4: Vall de Núria

On Sunday we went up to Vall de Núria, a high mountain valley in the Catalan Pyrenees. I believe we took the train. It rolled through a village at the foot of the mountains and then climbed into a rocky gorge.

At the top, the valley opened up: a frozen lake, the sanctuary buildings at the far end, and snow everywhere.

Getting to Vall de Núria today

The rack railway (Cremallera de Núria) is the main way into the valley, and vehicles are not allowed in. From Barcelona, take Rodalies line R3 to Ribes Enllaç and change to the rack railway there. If you come by car, park at Ribes Enllaç, Ribes-Vila or Queralbs and continue by train. A combined R3 and rack railway ticket is offered on weekends and holidays, so check availability and the boarding station when you book.

Back in Barcelona

In the evening we passed a Real Madrid shop on La Rambla, near the Boqueria market. A Cristiano Ronaldo number 7 shirt was on display.

Dinner that night was grilled salmon with rice.

Monday, December 5: Plaça de Catalunya

On Monday we had sushi at La Plaça, I believe the restaurant on the top floor of El Corte Inglés, which looks out over Plaça de Catalunya.

From the window, the whole square lay below us, with the Fnac sign across the plaza and the hills of Montjuïc in the distance.

Good to know today

La Plaça is a cafeteria-style gastro market on the top floor of El Corte Inglés on Plaça de Catalunya, known for its wide panoramic views of the city. Menus, prices and opening hours change, so check before you go.
