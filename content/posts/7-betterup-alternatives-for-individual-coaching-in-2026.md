7 BetterUp Alternatives for Individual Coaching in 2026

If you have been looking into BetterUp for personal or professional coaching, you may have noticed an important change in 2026: BetterUp is winding down its individual coaching program.

According to BetterUp, new individual subscriptions and coaching sessions are no longer available, and the BetterUp for Individuals program is scheduled to end on January 31, 2027.

That leaves an obvious question:

What should you use instead?

The answer depends on what you actually liked about BetterUp.

Maybe you wanted someone to help you make a difficult career decision. Maybe you wanted accountability. Maybe you wanted to become more confident, improve your relationships, handle uncertainty, or finally take action on something you've been thinking about for months.

And there is an important distinction here.

You don't necessarily need another platform that looks like BetterUp.

Sometimes you need a better coaching relationship.

Here are seven alternatives worth considering in 2026.

1\. Work directly with an independent coach

For many people, this is probably the most straightforward alternative. Instead of joining a large coaching platform, you find a coach yourself and work with that person directly. The biggest advantage is continuity. You know who you're working with. The coach gets to know you over time. You don't have to fit your situation into a platform's particular system or matching process. This can be especially valuable if your situation is complicated. Maybe you aren't simply trying to improve your leadership skills. Perhaps you're trying to figure out what you actually want from your career, whether you should make a major change, how to deal with uncertainty, or how to turn an idea into something real. In those situations, having one person who understands the context of your life can be more useful than having access to a large library of resources. The downside is that you have to do more of the selection

yourself. You should look carefully at the coach's background, approach, experience, pricing, and whether you actually feel comfortable talking to them. 2\. Use the ICF Credentialed Coach Finder

If credentials are important to you, the International Coaching Federation (ICF) has a searchable Credentialed Coach Finder.

You can filter coaches by credential, coaching themes, methods, experience, location, language, and rates.

This gives you something that a large platform can sometimes make difficult: the ability to compare individual coaches based on what you actually need.

For example, you might specifically look for someone with experience in:

3\. MentorCruise

MentorCruise is another option if what you want is a combination of coaching and mentoring.

The platform offers one-to-one mentoring, personal communication and calls, with mentors covering areas such as career development, leadership, startups and professional skills.

This can be particularly useful if your problem is connected to your career.

There is a difference between coaching and mentoring, though.

A coach will generally help you explore your own thinking and decisions.

A mentor may also bring their own experience into the conversation and say, "I've been through something similar, and this is what I learned."

CoachHub is heavily oriented toward organizations and employer-sponsored coaching. Its individual offering is presented around helping organizations deliver coaching to employees at scale.

6\. AI coaching tools

There is also another category that didn't really exist in the same way a few years ago: AI coaching.

AI can be useful when you want immediate access to something you can talk through.

You can describe a problem, ask questions, explore different perspectives and use it to organize your thoughts.

And it is available whenever you need it.

But I would be careful about confusing access with relationship.

An AI system can give you ideas.

It doesn't necessarily know you in the way that a human coach who has spoken with you repeatedly can.

It also can't replace every kind of professional help. If someone is dealing with a mental-health condition, trauma, abuse, or another serious clinical issue, coaching or an AI tool may not be the appropriate form of support.

For reflection, brainstorming and working through everyday decisions, however, AI can be a useful tool.

For deeper personal accountability and an ongoing human relationship, it is a different proposition. What should you ask before choosing a BetterUp alternative?

Before paying for coaching, I would ask a few basic questions:

1\. What exactly am I trying to change?

2\. What would success look like three or six months from now?

3\. Do I want advice, accountability, reflection, or some combination?

4\. Does this coach understand the problem I'm dealing with?

5\. Will I work with the same person consistently?

6\. Can I speak with the coach before committing to a package?

7\. What happens if I decide that coaching isn't the right fit?

Those questions can tell you much more than a list of platform features. By knowing as many details as possible it's easier to see the bigger picture.
