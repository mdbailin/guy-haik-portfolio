What is a life coach?

Life coaching is generally less about the coach having the answers and more about helping you work through your own situation.

A coach may help you clarify what you want, examine the choices in front of you, identify what is holding you back, and turn your intentions into action.

The coach doesn't necessarily need to have lived your exact life.

In fact, they probably haven't.

You may be dealing with a situation that is completely unique to you.

The coach's role can be to help you think about that situation more clearly. In 1958, psychologist Harry Harlow separated baby rhesus monkeys from their mothers and raised them with two artificial "surrogate" mothers. A wire mother equipped with a feeding bottle that provided milk. A cloth mother covered in soft terry cloth that provided no food. The baby monkeys overwhelmingly chose to spend most of their time clinging to the soft cloth mother, even when the wire mother was the one providing food. When hungry, infants would stretch briefly to the wire mother to feed, but immediately return to the security of the cloth mother. When exposed to loud, scary toys or new environments, the monkeys ran to the cloth mother for protection and comfort before daring to explore again. The study disproved the old idea that love and attachment grow merely from getting fed. Instead, it proved that physical touch and emotional security are basic, vital needs in infant development. So, which is better when you feel stuck?

There isn't one answer.

If you mainly want to learn from someone else's experience, a mentor may be the better choice.

If you need help understanding your own situation, making decisions, setting goals, or taking action, coaching may be a better fit.

And if you want both advice and space to think, you may not need to choose one label at all.
