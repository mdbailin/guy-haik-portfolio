What Is a Supportive Coaching Style?

A supportive coach places greater emphasis on listening, understanding, and helping you explore your thoughts before deciding what to do next.

Rather than immediately challenging your decisions, they might ask what matters most to you, what you're worried about, or how you've arrived at your current way of thinking. “It does not matter how slowly you go as long as you do not stop”- Confucius. Support can be reflected in understanding that slowing down isn't a bad thing. Marathon-like processes rather than fast sprints.

Which Coaching Style Is Right for You?

Your preferred style may depend on your personality, your circumstances, and what you're trying to work through.

You may prefer a direct approach if…

You are not sensitive.

You are not vulnerable.

You like to be criticized

You appreciate brutal honesty.

You don't believe in the politically correct thing.

You look for practical tools

You may prefer a supportive approach if…

You believe that the inclusive way is the infrastructure to things.

You are not into skipping any part that may be missing later on.

You feel like practical tools are given to the ones who have a longer journey.

You find things offensive sometimes.

You may prefer a combination if…

You want to feel understood but don't want someone to agree with everything you say.

You appreciate encouragement but also want honest feedback.

You sometimes need reflection and sometimes need help turning your thinking into action.

You want a coach who can adapt to the situation rather than follow one fixed approach.

These are only starting points. Preferring a supportive approach doesn't mean you can't handle direct feedback, and preferring directness doesn't mean you don't need empathy.

You may also find that your preferences change over time. The kind of support you need when making a major life decision may be different from what helps when you're working toward a clear goal. When Does a Coaching Style Become a Problem?

When someone is trying to manipulate you and to extract details the crafty way. For example In 2008, Elon Musk saw that classified data, emails, and reports from his company, Tesla, were being leaked. In order to trap the mole in his company, it was decided to write a long email classified "Top Secret" to all employees. The genius of this method is that the email sent to each employee is identical, except for one extra space. For each employee, the space was in a different place, and due to the length of the letter, the employees did not notice. When the letter was leaked, Elon Musk was able to identify who the mole was based on the gap in the specific location and fired him immediately. Elon Musk used a "Canary Trap" by embedding a unique binary code into each employee's email. This is just an example of what knowledge and strategy can cause.
