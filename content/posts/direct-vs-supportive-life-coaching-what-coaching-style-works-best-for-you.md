Choosing someone to guide you isn’t only about their qualifications, experience, or price. It is also about how they communicate with you.

When someone is too busy thinking about the definition of something, procrastination may take over. The point is to gain knowledge without being distracted from action. Thinking without acting changes very little.

## What is a direct style?

A direct guide communicates openly and gets to the point. They tell you what they think, even when it isn’t what you hoped to hear. If someone speaks with you openly, it can be a sign of a relationship built on trust. Someone who is very honest with you isn’t automatically bad company. Probably they want things to get done.

This style can fit you if you want clear opinions, practical suggestions, and someone who is willing to challenge you. The risk is that bluntness can feel like judgment, especially at a vulnerable moment.

## What is a supportive style?

A supportive guide puts more weight on listening, understanding, and helping you explore your thoughts before deciding what to do next. Instead of immediately challenging your decisions, they might ask what matters most to you, what you are worried about, or how you arrived at your current way of thinking.

Support can mean understanding that slowing down isn’t a bad thing. Some processes are marathons, not sprints. The risk is that it can become comfortable without moving anywhere.

## Can a guide be both direct and supportive?

Yes. The two approaches don’t have to be opposites. It is like a teacher who is firm with you, but from time to time fills the room with good vibes and humor. You can feel understood and still be told the truth.

## Which style is right for you?

Your preferred style may depend on your personality, your circumstances, and what you are trying to work through.

You may prefer a direct approach if:

- You appreciate honest feedback, even when it is uncomfortable.
- You are looking for practical tools and next steps.
- You don’t mind being challenged.
- You would rather hear it plainly than guess.

You may prefer a supportive approach if:

- You need to feel understood before you can change anything.
- You are going through something sensitive.
- You want to think things through at your own pace.
- Hard feedback too early makes you shut down.

You may prefer a combination if:

- You want to feel understood but don’t want someone to agree with everything you say.
- You appreciate encouragement but also want honest feedback.
- You sometimes need reflection and sometimes need help turning your thinking into action.
- You want someone who can adapt to the situation rather than follow one fixed approach.

These are only starting points. Preferring a supportive approach doesn’t mean you can’t handle direct feedback, and preferring directness doesn’t mean you don’t need empathy.

Your preferences may also change over time. What you need when making a major life decision may differ from what helps when you are working toward a clear goal. When I play video games, Fortnite for example, sometimes I like teamwork and sometimes I like to play solo. It doesn’t really matter, but if you can choose both, it might be better in my opinion.

## A short style check

If you are unsure what kind of communication works best for you, consider these questions.

**1. How do you usually respond to constructive criticism?**

- I appreciate clear, straightforward feedback.
- I respond best when feedback is delivered gently.
- It depends on the subject and how the feedback is given.

**2. Do you want to be understood first, or to get to the point first?**

If you want to get to the point as soon as possible, that is not a bad thing. It may mean you look for the point instead of analyzing everything and becoming overwhelmed. If you need to be heard first, that is fine too.

**3. Does the fear of what other people think hold you back?**

I believe it is one of the heaviest prisons we live in. It usually comes from worrying about being judged, criticized, embarrassed or rejected. When your decisions depend on outside approval, you hand over some control of your life to other people, and you lose some freedom. Most people are too focused on their own lives and challenges to spend much time judging yours. If you honestly don’t care what others think and you enjoy your life by your own rules, then both styles may be fine for you.

## When does a style become a problem?

Directness is not cruelty, and support is not dependence. A style becomes a problem when someone tries to manipulate you, humiliate you, pressure you, or extract details from you in a crafty way. If the way someone communicates leaves you smaller, more afraid, or less free, take that seriously, whatever the style is called. ## How I approach communication

There are some good people everywhere, and some bad people everywhere. The point is not to skip the conversation because you lost hope in the human race. Sometimes quiet people really do have a lot to say. They are just careful about who they open up to.

Some people keep their distance, and it doesn’t necessarily mean they don’t care or want to be alone forever. Sometimes it is because getting close once led to hurt, disappointment or betrayal, so distance feels safer. They may not be avoiding people. They may be protecting a part of themselves that was hurt before. This is my own observation, not a diagnosis, and I am not a therapist.

My suggestion is not to be anti-social, but to be selectively social. You are shaped by your environment, so choose it with care.
