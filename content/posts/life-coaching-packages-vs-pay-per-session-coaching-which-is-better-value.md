What's the difference between coaching packages and individual sessions?

The difference is fairly simple.

With pay-per-session coaching, you book and pay for each session separately. You can decide after each meeting whether you'd like to book another one, depending on the coach's availability and booking policies.

With a coaching package, you pay for several sessions together. The package may include a set number of meetings over a particular period, sometimes at a lower price per session than you'd pay individually.

Neither approach is automatically better. The important thing is to understand what you're paying for and whether the arrangement suits your needs. Coaching packages: Are they worth it?

One advantage of a coaching package is that it can give you a clearer structure. Instead of deciding whether to book another session every time, you have an arrangement in place that allows you to work on your goals over a period of time.

For some people, that structure is useful. Making changes in your life can take time, and having regular conversations may help you stay focused on what you want to achieve.

Packages can also be less expensive per session. If you already know the coach, feel comfortable with their approach, and want to work together over a longer period, paying for several sessions at once might make financial sense.

However, there are a few things worth considering before committing.

The downside of paying upfront

The biggest concern is that you're paying for sessions you haven't had yet.

What happens if you discover that the coach's approach doesn't suit you? What if your circumstances change, or you realize that you don't want to continue?

The answer depends on the coach's terms and conditions. Some packages may have expiry dates, cancellation restrictions, or limited refund options.

Before purchasing a package, ask what happens if you decide to stop. Make sure you understand the policy rather than assuming you'll be able to recover the cost of unused sessions.

Pay-per-session coaching: Is flexibility worth the extra cost?

Paying for one session at a time can be a good option if you're new to coaching or simply want to take things one step at a time.

You don't have to decide at the beginning how many sessions you'll need. You can have a conversation, reflect on what you've discussed, and then decide whether another session would be useful.

This approach can also help you get a sense of whether you and the coach are a good fit before making a larger financial commitment.

Of course, individual sessions have their disadvantages too.

The downside of paying for each session separately

If individual sessions cost more than sessions included in a package, the total can add up if you decide to work with the coach regularly.

You may also need to book each session separately, and availability isn't always guaranteed. Depending on the coach's scheduling arrangements, this could make it harder to maintain a consistent routine.

For some people, having to make a new decision each time can also make it easier to postpone the next session.

Still, paying more per session doesn't necessarily mean you're getting worse value. The flexibility may be worth the difference, particularly when you're still figuring out what you need. Here are a few questions worth asking yourself:

1\. What is a best friend?

A best friend is someone who loves you when you forget to love yourself.

2\. How much can I comfortably afford?

Think about the total amount you're being asked to pay, not just the price of each session. Don't stretch your budget for a package simply because the advertised rate looks attractive.

3\. Do I know whether this coach is right for me?

If you've never worked with the coach before, you may prefer to start with an individual session or an introductory consultation, if one is available.

4\. Am I ready to commit to a longer process?

You might have a clear goal and want regular support. Alternatively, you may be exploring a particular decision or trying to understand what you want to work on. Your circumstances can help determine which arrangement makes sense.

5\. What happens if I want to stop?

Check the cancellation policy, expiry dates, and refund terms before paying. These details can matter just as much as the advertised price.

6\. What am I actually getting from the sessions?

The number of meetings isn't the only thing that matters. Consider whether the conversations help you understand your situation, make decisions, and take meaningful steps forward.

Do you need a package to benefit from life coaching?

Not necessarily.

Some people benefit from ongoing coaching and prefer the consistency of a package. Others may find individual sessions more suitable, particularly if they want flexibility or have a specific issue they'd like to explore.

You also don't need to assume that you'll require a certain number of sessions before you've even started. Your goals, circumstances, and experience of coaching will influence what makes sense for you.

I'd be cautious about choosing a package simply because you feel that you must commit to a large number of sessions for coaching to work.

A commitment can be helpful, but it shouldn't replace your own judgment about whether the process is useful.

Good coaching should help you become better able to understand your situation and guide yourself. So, should you choose a package or pay per session?

If you already know the coach is a good fit and want consistent support, a package may be worth considering. If you're new to coaching, want more flexibility, or aren't ready to commit financially, individual sessions may be the better starting point.

Look at the total cost, read the terms, and think about what you actually need. Don't let a discount rush you into a decision you're not comfortable making.

And remember, choosing the most expensive option doesn't mean you're taking your development more seriously. What matters is whether the coaching is useful to you and whether the arrangement makes sense for your life.
