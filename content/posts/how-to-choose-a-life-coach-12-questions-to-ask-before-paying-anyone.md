How to Choose a Life Coach: 12 Questions to Ask Before Paying Anyone

Finding a life coach is easy.

Finding the right life coach is a different story.

There are thousands of coaches online, with different backgrounds, qualifications, prices, approaches, and promises. Some have years of experience. Some have impressive certifications. Some have built large personal brands. Others work quietly with a small number of clients.

So how are you supposed to know who is actually right for you?

You don't have to find the "best" life coach. You need to find someone who is a good fit for what you're trying to accomplish. Before you pay anyone, here are 12 questions worth asking.

1\. What do you actually help people with?

Start here.

A coach who says they help "everyone with everything" may not necessarily be the best choice for you.

Some coaches focus on career decisions. Others work with relationships, confidence, leadership, major life changes, accountability, or personal development.

You don't necessarily need someone who specializes in your exact problem. But you should have some idea of whether the coach regularly works with people facing situations similar to yours.

The important thing is whether their approach makes sense to you.

If you want someone who will mostly help you think things through, you may prefer one kind of coach. If you want someone who is more direct and willing to share perspectives or suggestions, you may prefer another.

"I don't want to pay someone just to ask me questions."

Good coaching isn't necessarily just a series of questions.

At the same time, a coach shouldn't automatically tell you what to do every time you have a problem. Sometimes the most useful thing they can do is help you see something you weren't seeing yourself.

The point isn't whether a coach gives advice.

The point is understanding how they use questions, perspective, suggestions and challenges during the coaching relationship. What should good coaching leave behind?

There is one question I think is worth asking beyond all the others:

What should I be able to do better because of coaching?

For me, the answer is simple.

Good coaching should leave you better able to guide yourself after the coaching ends.

That doesn't mean you'll never need another coach, therapist, mentor, friend, teacher, or other source of support.

It means the coaching shouldn't be about creating dependence.

The conversations should eventually become something you can carry with you.

You should become better at understanding your situation, asking yourself better questions, making decisions, recognizing patterns, and taking action.

The coach can help you get there.

So, how do you choose a life coach?

Most importantly, pay attention to the relationship.

You don't need to find the most famous coach.

You don't need the coach with the longest list of certificates.

And you don't need to commit immediately because someone tells you that you "need" coaching.

You need to find someone you trust, someone whose approach makes sense to you and someone who can help you work toward what you actually want from coaching.
