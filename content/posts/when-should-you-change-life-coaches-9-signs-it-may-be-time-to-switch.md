6\. You Feel Increasingly Dependent on Your Coach

This is an important point that deserves attention.

A coaching relationship should help you develop your own ability to understand situations, make decisions, and deal with challenges.

Of course, having someone to talk to and receive support from can be valuable. There is nothing wrong with appreciating your coach or wanting to continue working together.

However, if you increasingly feel unable to make decisions without consulting your coach, or believe you cannot manage your problems without regular sessions, it may be worth reconsidering the direction of the relationship.

Good coaching should not make you feel that you need someone else to manage your life.

The goal should be to develop your confidence, judgement, and ability to guide yourself, even when your coach is no longer involved.

8\. You Feel Pressured to Continue Paying for Sessions

Don't let anyone manipulate you and promise you things unattainable  to lure you slowly to his trap. A scientist once placed a frog into a part of boiling water. It will instantly jump out to save itself. It knew the danger. But then, the scientist tried something different. He placed another frog into a part of lukewarm water. The frog settled in comfortably. Then scientists slowly started heating the water. Just a little at a time. The frog felt the change, but it didn't see it as a threat. A few minutes passed. The water got warmer, and the frogs simply adjusted. The scientist kept increasing the temperature. Slowly, the water became dangerously hot, but by then, the frog had already gotten used to the heat, and before it realized what was happening, it was too late. Now, this is where the real game begins. This is called the Boiling Frog Effect. Smart manipulators slowly trap you. They don't start with major control. They start with small restriction, a small lie, a little fear. Then they slowly normalize it. People adjust. They accept the next change because it doesn't feel as bad as the last one. And by the time they realize how much they've lost, they've already become comfortable inside the trap, sir. When someone keeps changing the rules around you, don't just ask yourself if you can adjust. Ask yourself, where is this leading?

9\. The Need Issue Becomes Vague

There's a story of a village that was terrified of wild wolves. They would often come at night and steal the villagers' goats and chickens. The villagers collected money and bought a fierce and powerful hunting dog from another city. The dog kept a strict watch, day and night. The villagers fed it the best food from their own plates, built a good place for it to rest, and considered it the protector of the entire village. After two months of hard work, the dog risked its own life and killed the last wolf roaming around the village. Now, the village was completely safe. But then, something strange happened. As soon as the fear of wolves disappeared, the villagers stopped giving the dog good food. After a few days, they began feeding it left over scraps. And within a month, those very same villagers started throwing stones at the dog to drive it out of their streets because it's nighttime barking disturbed them and interrupted their sleep. This is exactly one of the bitterest truths about our value in real life and human selfishness. The world often values you not because of your respect, loyalty or kindness, but because of its own needs. The day someone's purpose is fulfilled or their fear disappears, all your sacrifices are thrown into the trash. This is human nature. Once the need is gone, even the greatest protector can begin to seem like a burden. Human nature = to appreciate something after losing it.
