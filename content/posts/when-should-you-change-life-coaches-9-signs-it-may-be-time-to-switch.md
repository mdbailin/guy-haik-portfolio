Talking with a guide starts with trust. Talking about trust is easy, though. So is the answer to trust nobody? No. The way I see it, sometimes a look in someone’s eyes is enough to sense whether they are telling the truth, and your gut feeling about a guide counts as information. But a feeling alone is not enough. Also, a good guide will sometimes challenge you, and that is not a problem in itself. The signs below are different: they are patterns that keep repeating.

Here are nine signs worth paying attention to.

## 1. You are encouraged to do things against your conscience

In combat sports, fighters follow their coach’s instructions in the ring, and that is fine there. Life is not a ring. A good guide can push you to grow, but should never push you to act against your values: to be aggressive, to cut people out of your life on a whim, or to lower yourself to someone else’s level, physically or verbally. Respect your character.

A good guide reminds you that you are going to be okay. You have made it through every hard day so far, and today is no different. If you leave conversations feeling pushed away from yourself, take that seriously.

## 2. Technology replaces human attention

These conversations work because a real person is present with you. Tools can support a guide, but warning signs are copy-pasted replies, generic templates, automated messages, or advice that clearly was not written with you in mind. If you feel that nobody is really listening, that is information too.

## 3. Talking has replaced doing

Conversations can become a comfortable place to talk about change without changing anything. If months go by and your life looks the same, ask yourself what you actually do in between. Go outside, take a trip, meet new people, try one small thing you have been avoiding. A good guide pulls you toward life, not away from it.

## 4. Conversations add noise instead of peace

Ever wondered why some people love being alone? Quiet people are not necessarily lonely. They often enjoy solitude because it gives the mind a break from constant noise, opinions and social pressure. They can think clearly, recharge, and enjoy their own company. And being alone doesn’t always mean wanting fewer people. Sometimes it means wanting better people.

Now apply that to guidance. After a conversation, do you feel clearer and calmer, or more anxious, agitated and full of other people’s opinions? A good guide leaves room for your own thoughts. Appreciate your “me time” and stay connected to nature.

## 5. Negativity or hatred is promoted instead of peace

Try to let go of hatred as much as you can, because it brings no benefit. A guide should help you understand anger and resentment, not feed them. Warning signs are someone who keeps encouraging you to see other people as enemies, to blame, to keep score, or to cut people off. Good guidance leaves you more peaceful, not more bitter.

## 6. You feel increasingly dependent on your guide

This is an important point that deserves attention. A relationship like this should help you develop your own ability to understand situations, make decisions and deal with challenges. Having someone to talk to can be valuable, and there is nothing wrong with appreciating your guide or wanting to continue.

However, if you increasingly feel unable to make decisions without consulting them, or believe you can’t manage your problems without regular conversations, it may be worth reconsidering the direction of the relationship. Good guidance should not make you feel that you need someone else to manage your life. The goal is to build your confidence, judgment and ability to guide yourself, even when your guide is no longer involved.

## 7. You can’t see meaning or progress

If the conversations drift into superficial things like gossip, or long stories about other people or about the guide, you are not in a good spot. Ask yourself: what has changed since I started? Can I name one insight, one decision, one new habit? If not, that is the sign.

Gossip has its own dynamic. People who talk about someone often don’t even enjoy each other’s company, and the only thing holding them together is their obsession with that person. So don’t stoop to that level, and don’t spend your time or money on conversations that go there.

## 8. You feel pressured to keep paying

Don’t let anyone manipulate you or promise things that can’t be achieved. There is the well-known metaphor of the boiling frog: a frog in slowly heated water gets used to each small change until it is too late. Real frogs tend to jump out as the water warms, so take it as a metaphor and not as science.

The metaphor describes how manipulation often works. It doesn’t begin with big control, but with a small restriction, a small lie, a little fear, each one normalized before the next arrives. Warning signs include pressure to buy long prepaid packages, guilt when you want a break, guaranteed results, terms that change along the way, and being told you are not ready to stop. A healthy relationship has clear prices, clear terms, and the freedom to pause.

When someone keeps changing the rules around you, don’t only ask yourself whether you can adjust. Ask yourself: where is this leading?

## 9. Your original need is met, or has become vague

There is a parable, widely shared and with no known author, about a village terrified of wolves that came at night to steal its animals. The villagers bought a fierce hunting dog, fed it the best food and treated it as the protector of the village. After two months it killed the last wolf. But once the fear disappeared, the villagers began feeding the dog scraps, and soon they were throwing stones at it because its barking disturbed their sleep.

It is a bitter parable about how the world often values us only as long as it needs us. In guidance it teaches two things. First, a good guide is not like the villagers: they recognize when the job is done and say so, instead of inventing new needs to keep you coming. Second, if you have outgrown a guide, or your original question has dissolved into “we just talk,” leaving is not betrayal. Thank them for the part they played and move on with respect.

## Before you switch, is there something worth discussing?

Before you leave, consider an honest conversation: say what is not working and what you need. A good guide will welcome that feedback and adjust. Blaming everyone and moving on to the next person solves little, and so does acting like a victim, but clear and calm feedback often solves a lot.

There is one exception. If the problem is pressure, disrespect, manipulation or crossed boundaries, you don’t owe anyone a debate. You can leave.

## How to find a better fit

Once you have decided to move on, take some time to consider what you actually want from the next relationship. Rather than choosing someone because of impressive marketing or a long list of testimonials, think about what was missing from your previous experience.

- What did I value in my previous guide, and want to keep?
- What was missing, or what bothered me?
- How will I know in a few conversations whether this new person is a better fit?

One more thing: guidance is not therapy. If you are going through serious emotional difficulty, a licensed mental-health professional is the right person to turn to. For more questions to ask, see [15 Questions to Ask Before Trusting a Spiritual Teacher or Guide](post.html?slug=15-questions-to-ask-a-life-coach-before-hiring-them).

## What I believe a good relationship should offer

I believe a good relationship starts with simple things. Simplicity leads to good things, and there is no point in making things complicated. It should also be obvious that color, age, religion, gender and race do not matter.

I also believe in practice that becomes natural. It is like a tornado kick: if you overthink it, it disturbs you, but when you have practiced enough, your brain already knows the motion and it happens on its own. Good guidance helps you build that kind of ease in how you think and decide.

And the relationship must rest on respect. If it loses the essence of respect, there may be no point in continuing. Be the reason someone still believes in goodness, and it may come back to you someday. That is how I understand karma: doing good without expecting anything in return.
