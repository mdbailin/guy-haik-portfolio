If you are going through a difficult period, you may find yourself asking a simple question: do I need a therapist, or would a spiritual guide be more helpful?

From the outside the two can look similar. Both involve talking with another person about your life, your thoughts, and what you want to change. But they are not the same thing, and the right choice depends largely on what you are trying to deal with.

In my NLP courses I was taught that “it depends” is very often the right answer. Almost nothing in life stays stable and can never be proven wrong, and this question is no exception.

## What a therapist does

Therapy focuses on mental health and emotional well-being. A therapist may help someone work through emotional distress, trauma, difficult experiences, relationship problems, anxiety, depression, or other psychological concerns.

Depending on their qualifications and where they practice, mental-health professionals may also diagnose and treat mental-health conditions. Therapy can look at the past, at patterns, at what is happening now, and at the future. The important point is that it is professional mental-health support.

## What a spiritual guide does

A guide focuses on meaning, direction, values and inner life, rather than on treating a condition. People look for one when:

- They are facing a big decision and want another perspective.

- They feel stuck, empty, or unsure what matters to them.

- They are going through a major transition.

- They want to understand what they actually want from the next stage of life.

- They want a calmer way of dealing with what happens to them.

A guide can ask questions, offer a perspective, and help you reflect. That doesn’t mean a guide has all the answers. I don’t think they should. A good guide helps you become better at finding your own answers. And to be clear: I am not a therapist.

## The biggest difference

One simple way to think about it: therapy addresses psychological and emotional difficulties, while a guide offers reflection and perspective on direction and meaning.

Real life isn’t always that tidy. Someone can function well and still benefit from therapy. Someone else can be doing reasonably well psychologically and still feel lost about their direction. And sometimes a person benefits from both.

## When therapy is the better choice

If you are dealing with significant emotional or psychological distress, trauma, or symptoms that may need professional treatment, speaking with a qualified therapist or other mental-health professional may be the right place to start.

You don’t need to convince yourself that conversations with a guide should be able to solve everything. Sometimes the most helpful thing you can do is get the right kind of professional support. A responsible guide will recognize this too, and will tell you so.

If someone makes you feel that their approach is the answer to absolutely everything, I would be cautious.

If you are struggling with your mental health or are in crisis, please contact a licensed professional or local emergency services.

## When a guide may be useful

Imagine you say:

- “I know what I value, but I keep drifting away from it.”

- “I have an idea of what I want to do, but I don’t know how to start.”

- “I made a decision, and I would like to think it through with someone.”

Those situations may suit a guide. You may not need someone to treat a problem. You may need someone to help you think clearly and take the next step.

## What if you are not sure?

This is probably the hardest situation. You know something isn’t working, but you don’t know what kind of help you need.

In that case, start with the problem rather than the service. Ask yourself:

- What am I actually trying to solve?

- Am I struggling with my mental health?

- Am I trying to process something that happened to me?

- Or do I know what I want, and I need perspective to move?

The answers can point you in the right direction. And if you still aren’t sure, it is fine to ask a qualified professional. You don’t have to figure everything out by yourself.

## Can you have both?

Yes, depending on your circumstances. Someone might work with a therapist on mental-health concerns while also talking with a guide about direction and meaning.

The roles should stay clear. A guide shouldn’t try to replace a therapist, and a therapist isn’t necessarily there to help you think through the bigger questions of your life. Different kinds of support serve different purposes.

## Finding what works for you

As a kid I found it hard to stay concentrated. Over time I found things that help me: physical activity (calisthenics), Rubik’s cubes, animals, nature, coffee, and especially music. These are my own experience, not a prescription, and they don’t replace professional care when it is needed. What a good guide can do is help you notice what works for you.

A Rubik’s cube also taught me something about approach. Before trying to solve a 4x4 cube, ask what the idea behind it is. Once you understand the point, the steps become much easier, and solving it has less to do with how clever you are than with knowing a pattern. How long would it take you to give up? A few hours? A few days? Before you try to solve any problem, look at the way you approach problems. The same goes for the questions inside us.

## What good guidance should leave behind

This is the part that matters most to me. Good guidance should leave you better able to guide yourself afterward. It shouldn’t make you dependent.

A guide might help you see a problem differently, ask a question you hadn’t considered, or point out a pattern. But the conversation ends, and you still have to make decisions and deal with difficult situations when nobody is there. If you leave more capable of doing that yourself, something valuable happened.

## So, which one do you need?

There isn’t a universal answer.

If you are dealing with a mental-health concern or significant distress, therapy or another appropriate mental-health service is probably the better place to start. If you are generally functioning well but want reflection on meaning, direction, or a decision, a spiritual guide may be worth considering. Sometimes the answer is both.

Don’t choose a guide just because you think you “should” solve it alone, and don’t choose therapy just because you are going through a hard time. Start with the problem, and then find the kind of support that fits it.
