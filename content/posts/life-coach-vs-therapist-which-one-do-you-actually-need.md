Life Coach vs Therapist: Which One Do You Actually Need?

If you are going through a difficult period in your life, you may find yourself asking a simple question:

Do I need a therapist or would working with a life coach be more helpful?

The two can sometimes look similar from the outside. Both involve talking with another person about your life, your thoughts, your problems, and what you want to change.

But they are not the same thing.

The right choice depends largely on what you are actually trying to deal with.

What does a therapist do?

Therapy is generally focused on mental health and emotional well-being.

A therapist may help someone work through things such as emotional distress, trauma, difficult experiences, relationship problems, anxiety, depression, or other psychological concerns.

Depending on their qualifications and the setting in which they practice, mental-health professionals may also diagnose and treat mental health conditions.

Therapy can involve looking at the past, understanding patterns, processing experiences, and dealing with what is happening in your life right now.

It can also involve thinking about the future.

The important point is that therapy is a form of professional mental-health support. "LONELINESS IS ONLY AN OPPORTUNITY TO CUT ADRIFT AND FIND YOURSELF. IN SOLITUDE YOU ARE LEAST ALONE. MAKE GOOD USE OF IT."

What does a life coach do?

Life coaching generally focuses on where you want to go rather than treating a mental-health condition.

For example, you might work with a coach because:

You are considering a career change.

You have a goal but struggle to follow through.

You are going through a major life transition.

You want more accountability.

You need help making an important decision.

You want to understand what you actually want from the next stage of your life.

You feel stuck and want another perspective.

A coach can ask questions, challenge your thinking, help you clarify your goals, and help you turn intentions into actions.

That doesn't mean a coach has all the answers.

So what's the biggest difference?

One simple way of thinking about it is this:

Therapy can help you address psychological and emotional difficulties. Coaching can help you work toward goals and changes you want to make in your life.

But real life isn't always that simple.

Someone can be completely functional and still benefit from therapy.

Someone can also be doing reasonably well psychologically and still feel lost about their career, relationships, direction, or future.

And sometimes a person may benefit from both. Restart is better than REGRET. Try to avoid situations that lead you to ask "what if". Don't call me Lucky. I failed more times than you tried.

When might therapy be the better choice?

If you are dealing with significant emotional or psychological distress, trauma, or symptoms that may require professional mental-health treatment, speaking with an appropriately qualified therapist or other mental-health professional may be the right place to start.

You don't need to convince yourself that you should be able to solve everything through coaching.

Sometimes the most helpful thing you can do is get the right kind of professional support.

When might coaching be useful?

Imagine that you say:

"I know I want to change careers, but I keep putting it off."

Or:

"I have an idea for something I want to do, but I don't know how to turn it into reality."

Or:

"I have made a decision, but I need someone to hold me accountable."

Those situations may be well suited to coaching.

You may not need someone to treat a psychological problem.

You may need someone to help you think clearly, define what you want, and actually take the next step. What if you are not sure?

This is probably the hardest situation.

You may know that something isn't working in your life, but you don't know what kind of help you need.

In that situation, start with the problem rather than the service.

Ask yourself:

What am I actually trying to solve?

Are you struggling with your mental health?

Are you trying to process something that happened to you?

Are you dealing with a major emotional problem?

Or do you know what you want but need help making it happen?

The answer can point you in the right direction.

And if you still aren't sure, it is okay to talk to a qualified professional and ask.

Can you have both a therapist and a life coach?

Yes, depending on your circumstances, it is possible to work with both.

For example, someone might work with a therapist to address mental-health concerns while also working with a coach on career goals or accountability.

The roles should remain clear.

A life coach shouldn't try to replace a therapist, just as a therapist isn't necessarily there to act as your personal accountability partner for every goal you have.

So, which one do you actually need?

There isn't a universal answer.

If you are dealing with a mental-health concern or significant psychological distress, therapy or another appropriate mental-health service may be the better place to start.

If you are generally functioning well but want help with goals, decisions, accountability, direction, or personal development, coaching may be worth considering.

And sometimes the answer can be both.

The important thing is not to choose coaching simply because you think you "should" be able to solve something yourself.

And you don't need to choose therapy simply because you're going through a difficult period.
