How to Find the Right Life Coach for You: A Practical Guide to Choosing

By Guy Haik

There is a song by Haddaway that begs for a sign about what is right and what is wrong. Choosing a coach can feel exactly like that. The honest answer is that it depends on who you are. The way I see it, every person who enters your life has a purpose: the good ones bring happiness, the difficult ones give experience, the worst ones teach unforgettable lessons, and the best ones leave beautiful memories. Some are a blessing and some are a lesson, but all of them help shape who you become. A coach is one of those people, so choose with open eyes. Here are nine things worth paying attention to.

1\. Start by Understanding What You Actually Need

The Beatles said all you need is love, and I truly believe the need for love is what all living things have in common. Justin Bieber sings about loving yourself, and of course there is nothing wrong with that. But before you look for a coach, get specific about what you need right now. Is it clarity about a decision, support through a transition, someone to hold you accountable, or simply an honest conversation? Write it down in one or two sentences. A coach can only be the right fit for a need you have named.

A line often attributed to Bruce Lee says that knowledge gives you power, but character earns respect. Keep that in mind: you are looking for both.

2\. Look Beyond Qualifications and Certificates

I try not to buy a shirt just because of the logo on it. Fancy brands make you pay for the name, not for the quality. Even a product people trust can come with strings attached: in 2017 Apple confirmed that software updates could slow down older iPhones with worn batteries, and many users felt it as a nudge to upgrade. The lesson is not about one company. It is that a polished image is not the same as what you actually receive.

The same goes for coaches. Training and certificates matter, so do ask where they trained and what code of ethics they follow. But then look at substance: how they listen, whether they say “I don’t know” when they don’t, and whether past clients speak well of them. The song Big City Life by Mattafix tells us not to let the system get us down. For me that means staying as detached as I can from the noise of the media and from other people’s rules about who is worth listening to.

3\. Compare Different Coaching Approaches

Not all coaches work in the same way. Some are structured and goal-driven, some are reflective and slow, some are tough, and some are spiritual. I really like everything related to Japan, so this reminds me of Miyamoto Musashi’s Dokkōdō, The Way of Walking Alone. He wrote its 21 principles shortly before his death in 1645, and they are a demanding blueprint for a focused mind. In my own words, they point to five ideas:

• Accept reality as it is. Suffering grows when we wish the world were different, so adapt to it instead of demanding that it adapts to you.

• Let go of ego and travel light. Possessions, titles and the need to impress make you fragile. If you need little, little can break you.

• Don’t be ruled by impulses. Chasing quick pleasure and emotional highs hands your life over to your moods.

• Own your path. Stop blaming bad luck or waiting for a miracle. Learn to stand tall, even in an empty room.

• Act. One technique mastered is worth a thousand theories. Guard your character and your integrity.

A samurai doesn’t fear death, he fears living without purpose. That hit deep, and it made me want to wake up with purpose.

But this is a tough, solitary path. Some people need a coach who pushes like that, and others need patience and gentleness. Neither is wrong. Ask each coach to describe how they usually work, then notice which style makes you feel braver and which makes you feel smaller.

4\. Pay Attention to Communication

Do you thank God for letting you wake up in the morning, just like yesterday? Why not? It doesn’t really matter what your religion is, I recommend starting. Many athletes openly thank God after a win, and I read it as a reminder that gratitude keeps a person humble in victory and steady in defeat. And if you speak only to God about what is on your heart, that is not a bad thing, but it is also worth having a human being to talk to.

That is where a coach comes in. In the first call, notice whether you feel heard. Do they listen more than they talk? Do they ask questions before giving advice? Do they let you finish your sentences? Communication is not a side feature of coaching, it is the work itself.

5\. Ask How They Approach Discipline and Progress

Eminem’s song Not Afraid is about doing something for yourself and not letting anyone who looks down on you stop you. It fits what I believe: most people fail not because they lack talent or opportunity, but because they lack discipline. They wait for motivation, quit the moment things get difficult, and get trapped in endless procrastination. Without discipline, talent and dreams can go to waste. Winners are not people who feel motivated every day. They act anyway. Discipline is not a mood, it is a firm decision to do what is necessary rather than what is comfortable.

Here is a practical way to build it:

• Remove the option of quitting. End the internal debate by deciding in advance that giving up is not on the table.

• Start small, but stay consistent. Tiny wins compound over time, more than sudden overnight transformations.

• Stop waiting to feel ready. Action comes first, motivation follows.

• Get comfortable with discomfort. Difficulty is exactly where growth happens.

• Track your progress. Keep an honest daily written record so you can’t lie to yourself about your effort.

Before this day ends, pick one difficult task for tomorrow and do it without excuses. Then, when you talk to a coach, ask how they handle accountability: how do they track progress, and what happens when you miss a week?

6\. Try Not to Let Unwelcome Guests In

I’m talking about hatred, and about people who try to promote it. Try to let this emotion go, because it leads to bad things. But don’t skip the conversation with a friend who hurt you without knowing it. Unspoken hurt takes on a life of its own and quietly damages relationships long after the original incident. Say a close friend forgets your birthday and you choose silence to keep the peace. The pain doesn’t disappear. You start keeping score inside, and even if that friend is wonderful to you for months, the unaddressed grudge shows up as late replies, passive-aggressive remarks and subtle withdrawal, leaving the other person clueless.

I like to think of it as inertia, the way Isaac Newton described it in 1687: something in motion keeps moving unless a force stops it. The letdown gives the first push, and replaying it in your mind adds momentum. The cost is real. In a 2001 study, psychologist Charlotte Witvliet and her colleagues found that dwelling on a past hurt with an unforgiving attitude produced stronger stress responses in the body, such as higher heart rate and blood pressure, than imagining forgiveness did. Silence doesn’t heal. It works as a slow, continuous force that pushes people away until the relationship is gone. I experienced it myself.

A good coach helps you notice these patterns without judging you. Ask how they work with difficult emotions and with conflict.

7\. Make the Most of What Is Offered to You

Do it generally, not only with coaching. It reminds me of the Foo Fighters’ song Best of You, which asks whether someone is getting the best of you. Part of it, as I see it, is never taking things for granted, which is a powerful life lesson.

There is a story about a father who handed his son 100 USD and said, “Take this money, go out and enjoy your day, but tonight I want you to tell me exactly how you spent it.” The son was excited. He went out with his friends, watched a movie, enjoyed some delicious burgers, and spent the day having fun. By evening he had only 10 USD left. He returned home, gave the remaining money to his father, and said, “Dad, I spent 90 USD today.” His father smiled and said, “That’s all right, son. But tomorrow I want you to work an entire day and earn just 10 USD. Then we’ll talk.”

The next morning the boy went looking for work. Under the scorching sun he worked wherever he could find an opportunity. His hands grew tired and his body became exhausted, but he kept going. By evening he had earned 10 USD. He came home proudly, handed the money to his father, and sat down. His father looked at it and said, “Son, let’s use these 10 USD to buy some burgers today.” The boy replied immediately, “No, Dad, please don’t spend this money. I worked the entire day under the hot sun just to earn it.” His father smiled, hugged him and said, “Now you understand, son. When money comes from your parents, spending it feels easy. But when you earn it through your own sweat, even spending a single dollar becomes difficult. Never take your parents’ money for granted. Every dollar they give you carries hours of work, sacrifice and responsibility.”

The same applies to a coach. Your time, your effort and your money are earned, so use every session fully: come with a question, take notes and follow through between sessions.

8\. Start Writing Things Down

For example, write down where you want to be five years from now. Get to know yourself better by playing association games (I believe games make us smarter). What goes through your mind when you hear the word excellence? They say it comes from daily habits and small, repeated actions rather than rare, massive changes, but what does it mean specifically to you?

I once read lines by Lord Byron, in Childe Harold’s Pilgrimage, that begin: “There is a pleasure in the pathless woods, there is a rapture on the lonely shore.” To me, rather than excellence, they mean Pura Vida, Spanish for pure life. Is there any way to have a pure, perfect or excellent life? No. The concept of Yin and Yang describes how life and the universe work through a dynamic balance of natural opposites, good and evil for example.

• Yin represents calm, coolness, darkness, rest, intuition, passivity and inward energy, associated with the moon and night.

• Yang represents action, warmth, light, movement, logic, activity and outward energy, associated with the sun and day.

A Chinese proverb from the Book of Han says that water that is too pure has no fish (水至清则无鱼, shuǐ zhì qīng zé wú yú). It is both a biological reality and a philosophical metaphor: completely sterile water cannot sustain life. In the same way, relationships and communities need tolerance for flaws, mistakes and differences in order to grow. A little mud in the water is what lets the fish live. Absolute purity is sterile, and a healthy life needs a soft, forgiving embrace of human imperfection.

By the way, perfection leaves no room for improvement. So in addition to writing things down, use a balance scale: list the pros and the cons, just to have a clearer picture. Bring those notes to your first call. A good coach will build on them.

9\. Remember That the Right Coach Should Help You Become More Independent

I used to think my mom was very strict, but seeing the current generation, I realize she saved me. I grew up with a single mother. When you have no apartment of your own and no financial backup, you don’t only learn to be alone and independent, you learn to survive. And when people write you off, the best answer is to prove them wrong.

Have you ever noticed that an ant can carry an object many times heavier than its own body? Estimates range from 10 to 50 times its weight, and sometimes more. An ant is tiny and easy to ignore, but it doesn’t let its size decide what it can accomplish. It struggles, falls, changes direction and keeps going until it arrives. Success works the same way. You don’t need to be the smartest, strongest or most talented person. You just need to keep moving when others give up. One small step may not seem important today, but repeated every day, small steps become something powerful. When your progress feels slow, remember the end. You don’t have to move fast, you just have to keep moving.

The right coach works toward making themselves unnecessary. If after a few sessions you feel more capable and less dependent, that is a very good sign.

There Is No Single Best Life Coach for Everyone

I truly believe in the individual side, but maybe all of us, or at least most of us, are looking for meaning. The Eurythmics sing that everybody is looking for something, and the most profound idea I know about it is Viktor Frankl’s principle in Man’s Search for Meaning. As someone who studied Political Science, my own answer is the feeling that you belong somewhere. Songs keep coming back to it: Linkin Park’s Somewhere I Belong, John Denver’s Take Me Home, Country Roads, and Coming Home by Diddy.

Just as Krishnamurti influenced Bruce Lee to look for freedom outside fixed martial arts forms, Frankl’s idea offers liberation from fixed psychological reactions. Many of Bruce Lee’s ideas did not come only from the training floor, but from his bookshelf. He read the Indian philosopher Jiddu Krishnamurti closely, who called truth a pathless land, and carried that idea into his martial art. He believed no single style, teacher or method could hold the whole truth for anyone, and he asked his students to look at themselves honestly, free of fixed patterns.

So which idea from a book quietly changed the way you live?

Questions to Ask Before You Choose

• What do I need right now, in one or two sentences?

• What training do you have, and what ethical code do you follow?

• How do you usually work, and what does a session look like?

• How do you handle accountability when I fall behind?

• How will we know whether this is working, and when would we end?

Could My Coaching Approach Be Right for You?

I’d say doing the best you can and not giving up is more than enough, and even a failure can become a good thing, because at least you tried. A line often attributed to Bruce Lee says that a goal is not always meant to be reached, it often serves simply as something to aim at.

There is a modern parable about a man who asked the Buddha what to do when he feels demotivated and nothing works. The Buddha answered that demotivation is like a cloud passing over the sun: it hides the light, but the sun is still there. A river that meets a rock doesn’t stop, it finds another way. A child doesn’t learn to walk without falling, and a bird doesn’t learn to fly without stumbling. Resting is not quitting, it is preparing with new energy. What you feel today is not permanent, and every great story has chapters of struggle. So be patient, be gentle with yourself, and keep moving forward, because giving up is easy, but rising again is where real power begins.
