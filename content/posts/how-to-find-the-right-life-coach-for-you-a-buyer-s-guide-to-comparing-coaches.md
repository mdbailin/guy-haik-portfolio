3\. Compare Different Coaching Approaches

One of the most important things to understand is that not all coaches work in the same way. I really like all the things related to Japan for example. And it reminds me I heard about Miyamoto Musashi’s Dokkōdō (The Way of Walking Alone) which is a brutal, masterclass blueprint for psychological invincibility. Written a week before his death, these 21 principles strip away the noise of modern comfort, emotional codependency, and ego, leaving behind a mindset built purely for survival and focus.

The core of his philosophy breaks down into five cold, operational realities:

1\. Radically Accept Reality (Rules 1, 6, 11)

Suffering happens when you wish reality looked different than it does. The rain falls, enemies strike, and plans shatter. Expecting the world to bend to your preferences is a guarantee of defeat. You cannot change the past, and you cannot dictate the terrain. Adapt to the world; do not demand that it adapts to you.

2\. Kill the Ego, Travel Light (Rules 4, 12, 14, 18)

Ego makes you fragile, slow, and easily offended. Cluttering your life with material possessions, titles, and fake security creates dead weight. A palace doesn't make a leader, and a cave doesn't break a beast. Your internal resilience is your only real fortress. If you need nothing, you cannot be bribed, blackmailed, or broken. Smash the ego. Say to yourself: ego is the enemy and I wish my enemy to be smashed.

3\. Starve Fleeting Impulses (Rules 2, 3, 5, 10, 13)

Comfort is a cage that dulls your edge. Living for short-term dopamine—whether through food, lust, or emotional highs—surrenders control of your life to your biology. Passion makes for a terrible captain. Operate from a place of absolute clarity, never on a partial feeling or a whim.

4\. Own Your Path Entirely (Rules 7, 8, 9, 15, 19)

Stop looking at other people's lives, stop blaming the universe for your bad luck, and stop waiting for a miracle or divine intervention. Separation, heartbreak, and death are inevitable laws of nature. You have to be entirely self-contained. Learn to stand completely tall, even in an empty room.

5\. Execution Over Everything (Rules 16, 17, 20, 21)

Do not be a collector of potential. One technique mastered and applied perfectly is worth a thousand theories. Death is the only guarantee in life, so fear of it is illogical. Guard your character and integrity above your physical safety. The final rule binds the rest: There are no days off from your own character.

For example, there's something called resentment inertia and it describes how unspoken hurt takes on a life of its own, quietly destroying relationships long after the original incident. When someone close hurts you (like a best friend forgetting your birthday) choosing silence to "keep the peace" doesn't actually erase the pain. Instead, you start keeping score inside. Even if that person is wonderful to you for months afterward, the unaddressed grudge triggers passive-aggressive behavior, late replies, and subtle emotional withdrawal, leaving the other person completely clueless. The concept mirrors Isaac Newton’s 1687 law of inertia: an object in motion stays in motion unless stopped by an outside force. The initial letdown provides the first push, and replaying it in your mind adds momentum. This silent grudge comes at a heavy personal cost. A 2001 psychological study by Charlotte Witvliet showed that replaying old grievances triggers the same spike in heart rate and blood pressure as if the event were happening in real time, whereas practicing forgiveness physically calms the body. Ultimately, silence does not heal, it acts as a slow, continuous force that pushes people away until the relationship is gone. I experienced it myself.
