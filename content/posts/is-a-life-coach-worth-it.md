If you are looking for guidance, you may have already asked yourself one simple question: do I actually need someone to guide me?

It is a fair question. There are plenty of people offering guidance today, from teachers and mentors to coaches and online gurus. Some ask for little, while others charge hundreds of dollars a session or sell packages worth thousands. And when you are about to invest your time, trust or money, you don’t just want a nice conversation. You want to know whether it will actually help.

The honest answer is that guidance isn’t for everyone. It depends on the question you are trying to answer, what you expect, and whether you are willing to do something with what you discover.

Here are seven situations where guidance may, or may not, be worth seeking.

## 1. You Know You Want a Change, but Not What Comes Next

This is one situation where guidance can be useful. You may know that something in your life isn’t working, but you can’t clearly see the next step.

Many people today feel overwhelmed. Every hour is rush hour, there is little time in nature, and no one around to share a bit of wisdom. If you feel lost, maybe it is because of that.

Bob Marley was once asked in an interview whether he had made a lot of money from his music, and whether he was a rich man. He answered with a question: what do you mean by rich? Possessions, he said, don’t make you rich, and his richness is life forever. In other words, you are not rich until you have something that money cannot buy.

Maybe you are unhappy with your career. A former UFC champion liked to say that he doesn’t fight for money but for legacy. What does that mean? It means having something to leave behind after you are gone, a bit like a kingdom or a dynasty. Ethnic groups, for example, are held together by something shared: a feeling that they belong somewhere, with the same culture, language and vision. In the end, you wish to be where you belong.

Maybe your old goals no longer feel meaningful. Then challenge yourself and push beyond your limits, and wake up your inner child, because children use their imagination more than we do. Bruce Lee is often quoted as saying that if you believe something is impossible, you make it impossible. I don’t need thousands of people who made it. I just need to know that one person did, to realize it can be done.

Maybe you keep going back and forth about a major change. Change is not necessarily bad, and the way we speak to ourselves shapes how we see our life. Sometimes the problem isn’t a lack of motivation but a lack of clarity. You tend to follow the majority instead of the right way, and “listen to your heart” can sound like just a common phrase. But if you can’t express what you feel, it is hard to know what you want. Children express feelings naturally: when they are sad, they cry, and when they are happy, they smile.

It also helps to choose a target. In the movie American Sniper, a character says that if you aim small, you miss small. Aim at the center, not at the edge, and ask yourself which target you are aiming for, and whether the odds are in your favor.

A guide can help you step outside your usual way of thinking, ask questions you haven’t considered, and turn a vague problem into something concrete. Maybe you are not even aware of what the real problem is, and you are facing an opponent without knowing who it is. If something is buried deep in your soul, you need a place where you feel free to talk about it. Spirituality and words of wisdom can help: the Beatles’ song Let It Be speaks of Mother Mary coming in times of trouble with words of wisdom.

But there is an important distinction. A guide shouldn’t make every decision for you. There is such a thing as a journey of self-discovery. In the film Into the Wild, the main character went off alone toward Alaska, leaving behind his money and identity documents. I don’t offer that as a model, because his story ended in tragedy. But his point stands: you often have to step out of your comfort zone and discover things yourself. You won’t find hidden gems by following only what is written online. The purpose of guidance is to help you understand your own situation, so that you can make better decisions yourself.

## 2. You Keep Thinking About Changing, but Never Take Action

Have you ever known exactly what you should do, and still didn’t do it? You tell yourself you will start tomorrow, make a plan, feel motivated, and a few days later nothing has changed. Then the cycle begins again.

This is where accountability has real value. When you work toward something alone, it is easy to make excuses. When you have told another person what you are going to do, it is harder to ignore. A good guide can help turn “I should probably do this” into “this is what I am going to do this week.”

A guide can’t take the action for you, but having someone who follows up can make a difference. Think of a child whose parents work around the clock and give him money instead of attention. Money doesn’t fill the void, and children are not stupid: they understand what is happening. Or think of a strict teacher who clearly cares, and who often earns more respect than one who doesn’t.

People today are so distracted that they miss these things. How often do you tell someone you love that the world is brighter with them in it? Don’t assume it is obvious. It is human nature to appreciate something only after losing it. If you need help not falling into that trap, that is completely fine. You are not the only one.

## 3. You Are Going Through a Major Life Decision

Some decisions are difficult because there is no obvious right answer. Should I stay in my career? Should I move somewhere else? Should I start something of my own? Should I continue with a relationship or make a change?

Friends and family can give advice, but they also have their own opinions, experiences and expectations. A guide can offer a different kind of conversation: not telling you what they would do, but helping you understand what you want and why. That is valuable when you feel pulled in several directions.

For people who trust in God, it can be easier to accept that the script was already written, what some call destiny. A current UFC champion often credits God after his fights. The story of Joseph, the eleventh son of Jacob, points the same way: he went through betrayal and prison, and understood much later that it had all happened for a reason. Everything that happens, happens for a reason.

And remember that you are a product of your environment. Surround yourself with good people, educated people, and, most importantly, well-mannered people.

## 4. You Want Someone to Challenge Your Thinking

Sometimes we don’t need more information. We need someone to challenge how we are thinking. We become very good at explaining why we can’t: “I don’t have enough experience.” “It’s too late.” “I don’t have enough money.” “I’ll start when things settle down.” Some of these may be true, but some are assumptions we have repeated so often that they feel like facts.

A good conversation can test those assumptions, not by pretending every obstacle is imaginary, but by asking: is this actually impossible, or is there another way to look at it?

Have you ever asked why some people turn to alcohol or drugs? Often it is escape: they prefer to run from their problems rather than face them. Everybody has issues, and there is no such thing as perfect. (If you are struggling with addiction, please seek professional help.)

In The Lion King there is a scene where Rafiki and Simba talk about exactly this. Simba says he knows what he has to do, but going back means facing the past he has run from for so long. Rafiki hits him with a stick, and when Simba complains that it hurts, Rafiki answers that the past can hurt, and you can either run from it or learn from it. Simba takes the stick and goes back.

## 5. You Already Know What You Need to Do

This might sound strange, but guidance may not be necessary. If you know what you want, have a realistic plan and are consistently taking action, you may not need anyone to tell you what you already know. Sometimes the best investment is simply doing the work.

A guide shouldn’t create dependency. If every decision requires another conversation, something has gone wrong. Good guidance helps you become more capable of moving forward on your own.

In the anime Naruto there is a scene where Kakashi ties Sasuke up so that he won’t do anything foolish while seeking revenge. Kakashi tells him that revenge never ends well and only leaves emptiness. Sasuke shouts back that Kakashi can’t understand, and that maybe he would if Sasuke took everyone important from him. Kakashi quietly answers that he is too late for that, because those people are already gone. Kakashi acts more like a mentor than a teacher or instructor. The point is: don’t let anger control you, and learn to be alone and independent.

## 6. You’re Looking for Someone to “Fix” Your Life

This is probably a situation where guidance isn’t worth it. No one can fix your life for you. They can’t make your decisions, control other people, or guarantee that you will get the job you want or that your relationship will work. And they can’t promise that you will become a different person in a few weeks.

If someone sells guidance as guaranteed transformation, be cautious. A good guide can offer support, structure, perspective and a place to think honestly. But you still have to do the work. Bob Marley’s Redemption Song says as much: nobody but ourselves can free our minds.

## 7. You’re Dealing With a Problem That Needs a Different Professional

This is an important boundary. Spiritual guidance is not therapy, medical care or financial advice. If someone is experiencing serious mental health difficulties, a qualified mental health professional is the right person. If you are in crisis, please contact local emergency services or a crisis line. A responsible guide can recognize the limits of what guidance can provide.

At the same time, it is no shame to ask for help or be advised. In Kill Bill Vol. 2, the main character goes to a master to be trained. In Star Wars, Luke Skywalker goes to Yoda to be trained hard. In Dragon Ball Z, Goku goes to King Kai to train before facing a new opponent. The lesson is the same: it is not a shame to ask for help, to be trained, or to be advised by someone.

So, Do You Need Guidance?

There isn’t a universal answer. For one person it could be unnecessary. For another, it can provide the clarity and accountability they have been looking for for years. The better question is not whether guidance is worth it in general, but whether it would help with the particular question you are dealing with.

Start there. What exactly are you struggling with? What would you like to be different? Have you tried to solve it yourself? What is stopping you? And what would meaningful progress look like? If you can’t answer these, you may need more time to understand the problem before asking anyone to help.

But if you know what you are struggling with and want another perspective, some structure, or someone to hold you accountable, guidance may be worth exploring. How will you ever know if you never even try? A line often attributed to Einstein says that a person who never made a mistake never tried anything new. He is also often quoted as saying that if he had an hour to solve a problem, he would spend most of it defining the problem.

How to Tell If a Guide Is Right for You

Before committing to anything, have a conversation. Explain what you are dealing with. Ask how they work, and what they think guidance could realistically help you with. And pay attention to how they respond. Do they listen? Do they ask meaningful questions? Do they make unrealistic promises? Do they seem more interested in understanding you, or in selling you something?

Be especially careful with these red flags: demands for obedience, pressure to cut ties with people close to you, large payments, and claims to hold the only truth. You don’t have to choose someone after one conversation. Take your time.

A saying often attributed to Confucius is that our greatest glory is not in never falling, but in rising every time we fall. Even if you can’t tell yet which guide is right for you, you have gained the experience of rising after falling. Move on, let go and leave the past behind, because there is no point in complaining.

The Real Return on Investment

The value of guidance shouldn’t be measured only by how good a conversation feels. Feeling understood is valuable. But if you want your life to change, there should eventually be some movement outside the conversation. Maybe you make a decision you have been avoiding, take action on a goal, understand a recurring pattern, stop wasting time on something that isn’t right for you, or become more confident about a direction.

The result doesn’t have to be dramatic. Progress is still progress. And sometimes the most valuable result is not being given an answer, but becoming clearer about the answer you already had inside you. Beyond everything else, ask yourself: do I feel better with myself? Do I love myself more? Am I more open-minded than before? Can I see things from a different point of view? And what goes on in my mind when I hear the word love?

Is Guidance Right for You?

You don’t need to believe that guidance is the answer to every problem, and you don’t even need to be certain that you need it. If you are curious, start with the question. What are you trying to change? What have you already tried? Where are you stuck? And what kind of help would actually be useful?

A line often attributed to T. S. Eliot says that only those who risk going too far can find out how far one can go. If that speaks to you, you are probably headed in the right direction. And Bruce Lee said to empty your mind and be formless and shapeless, like water. People say he became so wise partly because he read philosophy. Before starting any process, you have to find it interesting enough to proceed.
