BetterUp vs. a Private Life Coach: Which Is Better for Personal Growth?

If you have been using BetterUp as an individual and have built a good relationship with your coach, you may be wondering what happens next.

BetterUp's individual coaching program is winding down, with the program scheduled to end on January 31, 2027. For people who have spent months working with the same coach, this can feel like more than the end of a subscription.

You may have built a relationship with someone who understands your goals, your problems, your personality and the things you have been trying to change.

So naturally, the first question is:

What do I do now?

But I think there is another question worth asking before looking for a replacement:

What was I actually getting from coaching and what do I want to keep?

That question changes the way we look at the choice between a coaching platform and a private life coach.

You don't necessarily need another BetterUp

When a service ends, the natural reaction is to look for the closest replacement.

It makes sense. If you are used to one system, why not simply find another system that works in a similar way?

But personal development is not necessarily about finding the same product again.

Maybe what you valued was accountability.

Maybe it was having somebody challenge your thinking.

Maybe you appreciated having regular conversations about your goals.

Maybe you simply needed somebody who remembered what you had been working on and could help you continue the conversation.

Or perhaps the most valuable part was having a place where you could think out loud without being judged.

Once you understand what you actually valued, you can look for that specifically.

And that might lead you to another platform.

Or it might lead you to a private coach.

Or it might lead you to something completely different.

The platform is not the progress

One thing I think is important to remember is that your progress does not belong to BetterUp.

The platform was simply where some of your conversations happened.

What you learned belongs to you.

The decisions you made belong to you.

The habits you developed belong to you.

The questions you learned to ask yourself belong to you.

This is why the end of a coaching service does not necessarily mean starting from zero.

Think about learning to solve a Rubik's Cube.

You do not necessarily need extraordinary intelligence to solve one. You need to understand the algorithms and perhaps more importantly, know the order and timing in which to use them. Similar to succeeding doing a muscle-up, you just need to know how to be in sync with the motion and to combine it with the right timing(a bit similar to the game “Guitar Hero”). Similar to succeeding using nunchaku, you know when you need to release the nunchaku so the motion keeps going and the nunchaku embodies the infinity symbol.

In a strange way, coaching can sometimes work similarly.

A coach may help you recognize the patterns, questions, habits or "algorithms" that help you deal with your own problems.

The goal should not be that you need the coach forever to tell you what to do.

The goal should be that eventually you recognize some of those patterns yourself.

This is also why discipline matters.

As Warren Buffett has famously put it, We don't have to be smarter than the rest. We have to be more disciplined than the rest. Discipline is one of the aspects required if you wish to make it in life no doubt. 

Coaching can give you insight, but insight still has to become action.

BetterUp and a private coach offer different experiences

A coaching platform and a private life coach are not necessarily trying to provide exactly the same experience.

A platform can offer structure and convenience.

There may be a system for finding a coach, scheduling sessions, setting goals, completing assessments, communicating between sessions and accessing additional resources.

For someone who likes structure, that can be very useful.

A private life coach is different.

Instead of entering an established platform, you are choosing an individual person to work with directly.

That can give you more responsibility over the relationship, but it can also give you more freedom.

You can decide what kind of person you want to work with.

You can look for someone whose personality fits you.

You can ask about their approach.

You can discuss your specific situation before deciding whether you want to continue.

And if the relationship works well, you may develop much more personal understanding with that individual.

Neither approach is automatically better.

The important question is which approach fits what you actually need.

The relationship can matter more than the platform

One of the biggest advantages of having worked with a coach for a long time is that you do not have to explain your entire life from the beginning every time you have a conversation.

Your coach may already know your goals.

They may know what you tried.

They may remember the problems you were dealing with six months ago.

They may notice changes that you do not notice yourself.

That continuity can be extremely valuable.

So if you have had a particularly good relationship with your BetterUp coach, there is nothing wrong with asking what they plan to do after their BetterUp engagement ends.

Your coach may have another professional practice, another platform they use or way for clients to continue working with them independently.

Of course, you should not assume that this will be possible. Every coach's situation is different.

But it is worth asking.

And if continuing with that particular coach is not possible, the relationship can still teach you something important about what you want from your next coach.

A private coach gives you more responsibility and more choice

If you decide to work with an independent coach, you are no longer simply choosing a platform.

You are choosing a person.

That means you may want to think about what you actually need help with.

Do you need help with:

Career decisions?

Personal development?

Confidence?

Relationships?

Leadership?

Accountability?

A major life transition?

Finding direction?

Changing habits?

Making a difficult decision?

"Personal development" can be too broad.

The clearer you can be about the problem, the easier it becomes to find someone who can actually help("If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough" – Albert Einstein ).

You should also think about how you prefer to be coached.

Some coaches mainly ask questions and help you reach your own conclusions.

Others are more comfortable sharing frameworks, ideas, suggestions or practical exercises.

Neither approach is automatically superior.

The question is what actually helps you.

What should good coaching leave behind?

This is probably the most important question in the entire discussion.

For me, good coaching should leave you better able to guide yourself after the coaching ends.

That does not mean you should never need a coach again.

There are situations in life when another person's perspective, experience or support can be extremely valuable.

But the coaching should ideally change something about the way you deal with your own life.

For example, imagine somebody begins coaching by saying:

"I hate my job."

That statement is real, but it is also vague.

A useful conversation might help the person break the problem down.

Do they hate the actual work?

Their manager?

The hours?

The salary?

The lack of purpose?

The lack of freedom?

Or is something happening in their personal life that is making the job feel worse?

Eventually, a question that originally came from the coach may become a question the person asks themselves.

That is progress.

The visible product of coaching is the conversation.

The more lasting product may be what happens between conversations.

Sometimes the best next step is not another coach

There is another option that people often overlook:

Take a break.

You do not necessarily have to replace BetterUp immediately.

Perhaps you have already made significant progress.

Perhaps you need time to apply what you have learned.

Perhaps you want to find out whether you can continue independently.

That is completely reasonable.

Ask yourself:

What did I learn from coaching that I can now put into practice on my own?

If the answer is "quite a lot," perhaps you do not need another coach immediately.

You can always look for one later if you discover that you need additional support.

Don't confuse activity with progress

Modern life gives us an enormous number of things competing for our attention.

Phones.

Social media.

Notifications.

Endless scrolling.

Constant comparison.

It is easy to become busy without necessarily moving forward.

Sometimes the answer to a complicated problem is surprisingly simple.

Maybe you need better priorities rather than more time.

Maybe you need to spend less time looking at a screen and more time exercising. Maybe you need to meet new people.

Maybe you need to spend more time outside.

Maybe you need to return to activities that made you feel alive before everything became an app.

In many countries in Southeast Asia, people live simple lives. Simplicity. So, back to basics and back to how it was before civilization controlled our life? yes. Stop scrolling or tapping or comparing one device to another and doing stuff like this. Go out to meet new friends(magic moments) and spread some good vibes. A good coach will let you know we don't really need much to be truly satisfied and happy and it's not about money and materialism it's about love.

What should you look for in your next coach?

If you decide that you want another coach, I would start with five questions.

1\. What problem am I actually trying to solve?

Be specific.

Do not simply say "I want personal growth."

What does that mean for you?

What would actually be different if coaching worked?

2\. Do I want questions, advice or both?

Some people need somebody to help them discover their own answers.

Others want practical suggestions and frameworks.

Knowing what works for you can make the coaching relationship much more productive.

3\. Do I want short-term help or an ongoing relationship?

Sometimes you need three conversations about one difficult decision.

Sometimes you want somebody who will work with you for six months or longer.

Neither is inherently better.

You simply need to know what you are looking for.

4\. Can I speak with the coach before committing?

If possible, have an introductory conversation.

Ask yourself:

Do I feel listened to?

Can I explain what I am struggling with?

Does the person understand what I am trying to accomplish?

Do they challenge my thinking in a useful way?

Do I feel comfortable being honest with them?

Coaching is a relationship.

Sometimes you simply do not know whether a relationship works until you have a conversation.

5\. What happens if it isn't working?

You should not feel trapped in a coaching relationship.

Ask about cancellation, changing the arrangement and what happens if you decide that the relationship is not a good fit.

Sometimes a relationship simply does not work.

That is not necessarily a failure.

You learn something, let go and move forward.

Don't try to recreate BetterUp exactly

There can be a temptation to search for "the next BetterUp."

I would resist that.

BetterUp was one particular way of delivering coaching.

Your next coaching relationship does not have to look the same.

Maybe you want more continuity.

Maybe you want longer conversations.

Maybe you want someone who knows your situation personally.

Maybe you want a coach who works with fewer clients and can give you more individual attention.

Or maybe you realize that you do not need coaching as often as you thought.

This is where adaptability matters.

Bruce Lee's famous "be like water" philosophy is useful here: instead of becoming attached to one particular form, learn to adapt to changing circumstances.

The same principle can apply to coaching.

The purpose is not to remain loyal a particular platform.

The purpose is to keep moving forward.

You don't have to start over

The end of BetterUp's individual coaching program may be disappointing, especially if you have developed a strong relationship with your coach.

But it does not have to mean the end of your personal development.

In fact, it can be a useful moment to ask a more fundamental question:

What do I actually need help with now?

Maybe the answer is another coaching relationship.

Maybe it is a different kind of professional support.

Maybe it is a few months of putting what you have already learned into practice.

There is no universal answer.

What matters is that the next step is chosen deliberately rather than simply because a platform you were using has ended.

Good coaching should not leave you dependent on a platform.

Ideally, it should leave you with something more valuable:

A better understanding of yourself.

Better questions.

Better habits.

Better judgment.

And ultimately, a greater ability to guide yourself.

A simple transition plan

If you are currently using BetterUp, the practical steps are fairly simple:

Check your actual subscription end date.

Save the coaching notes, messages, assessments and resources you want to keep.

Write down the three most important things you learned from your coaching.

Write down the problems or goals you still want to work on.

Ask your current coach whether continuing independently is possible.

If it is not, speak with two or three potential coaches before choosing one.

Do not choose someone simply because they are the closest replacement for BetterUp.

Choose someone who fits where you are going next.

The last point may be the most important.

You are not looking for a new platform to store your progress.

You are looking for the kind of support that makes sense for the person you are becoming.

BetterUp may be ending one particular service.

But your personal development does not have to end with it.

The real question is not simply:

"What should replace BetterUp?"

It is:

"What do I need now and what have I already learned that I can carry forward?"

That answer may lead you to a private life coach.

It may lead you to another coaching platform.

Or it may lead you to discover that, for the moment, you are ready to guide yourself.

And perhaps that is one of the most valuable things good coaching can leave behind.
