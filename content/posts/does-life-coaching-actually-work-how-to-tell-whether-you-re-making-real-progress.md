Does Life Coaching Actually Work? How to Tell Whether You're Making Real Progress

If you've ever considered working with a life coach, you may have wondered whether it actually makes a difference.

It's one thing to sit down with someone, talk about your problems, and leave feeling that you've had an interesting conversation. It's another thing to see something change in your life afterward.

Maybe you want to make a decision you've been putting off. Maybe you keep finding yourself in the same situations and can't work out why. Or perhaps you know something needs to change, but you haven't figured out what to do about it.

In any of these situations, it's reasonable to ask what coaching can actually do for you.

The honest answer is that life coaching can be useful, but it isn't a guarantee of change. And the fact that you're having regular sessions doesn't automatically mean you're making progress. So how can you tell whether it's working?

Does life coaching actually work?

It can, but it depends on what you're hoping to get from it, how you work with your coach, and what happens beyond the conversations themselves.

Sometimes, talking through a situation with someone who isn't directly involved can help you see it differently. You might recognise an assumption you've been making, realize that you've been avoiding a decision, or understand why you keep approaching a problem in the same way.

That kind of clarity can be valuable. But clarity on its own doesn't always lead to change. Here's four years of therapy in 20 seconds. Fear shows you what matters. Anger shows you where a boundary was crossed. Anxiety means you're living in the future. Motivation fades, habits change your life. Your past is a chapter, not your whole story. Trigger shows you what still needs healing. Control is an illusion. Your thoughts shape you. Your habits build you and your choices define you.

You may understand exactly what you want to do and still find it difficult to take the first step. You may leave a session feeling motivated, only to fall back into your usual routine a few days later.

That's where it becomes important to look beyond how a session felt and consider what, if anything, is changing.

Coaching isn't a magic solution, and no coach can guarantee a particular result for every client. What matters is whether the process is helping you understand your situation, make decisions, and take steps that are meaningful to you.

Why good conversations don't always lead to progress

Have you ever had a conversation that felt helpful at the time, but a week later you realised that nothing had really changed? This can happen in coaching, too. The way I see it, conversion helps but the things that lead to progress are discipline, hard work, technique, patience, humility, and continuous improvement.  This is simply how you become stronger, it's not about having more power.

You might spend several sessions discussing the same difficulty. Each conversation gives you something new to think about, but you keep returning to the same problem without doing anything differently.

There's nothing wrong with taking time to understand a situation. Sometimes you need to talk things through before you're ready to act. But if the conversations continue without a clearer direction, it's worth asking what you're hoping to get from them.

Are you understanding the problem better? Are you considering options you hadn't thought about before? Are you making decisions, testing new approaches, or learning something about how you respond to challenges?

Or are you simply talking about the same things without getting anywhere?

These aren't always easy questions to answer, especially when you've invested time, money, and trust in a coaching relationship.

Still, you should be able to raise them with your coach. You shouldn't have to pretend that things are working just because you're paying for the sessions.

So, does life coaching work?

Life coaching can be useful, but its value isn't measured by how many sessions you attend or how inspiring a conversation feels.

Look at what you're learning, the decisions you're making, the actions you're taking, and whether you're becoming better able to handle challenges in your own life.

If you're not seeing progress, talk about it. Revisit your goals, ask questions, and consider whether the approach needs to change.

And if coaching no longer serves a useful purpose, it's okay to reconsider your commitment.
