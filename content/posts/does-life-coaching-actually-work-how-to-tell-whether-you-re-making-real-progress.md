If you have ever considered talking with a spiritual guide, you may have wondered whether it actually makes a difference.

It is one thing to sit down with someone, talk about your problems, and leave feeling that you had an interesting conversation. It is another thing to see something change in your life afterward.

Maybe you want to make a decision you have been putting off. Maybe you keep finding yourself in the same situations and can’t work out why. Or perhaps you know something needs to change, but you haven’t figured out what to do about it. In any of these situations, it is reasonable to ask what guidance can actually do for you.

The honest answer is that it can be useful, but it isn’t a guarantee of change. And the fact that you are having regular conversations doesn’t automatically mean you are making progress. So how can you tell whether it is working?

## Does it actually work?

It can, but it depends on what you hope to get from it, how you take part, and what happens beyond the conversations themselves.

Sometimes, talking through a situation with someone who isn’t directly involved can help you see it differently. You might recognize an assumption you have been making, realize that you have been avoiding a decision, or understand why you keep approaching a problem in the same way.

That kind of clarity can be valuable. But clarity on its own doesn’t always lead to change. You may understand exactly what you want to do and still find it difficult to take the first step. You may leave a conversation feeling motivated, only to fall back into your usual routine a few days later.

That is where it becomes important to look beyond how a conversation felt and consider what, if anything, is changing. No guide can guarantee a particular result. What matters is whether the process helps you understand your situation, make decisions, and take steps that are meaningful to you.

## Why good conversations don’t always lead to progress

Have you ever had a conversation that felt helpful at the time, but a week later you realized nothing had really changed? This can happen here too. The way I see it, conversation helps, but the things that lead to progress are discipline, hard work, technique, patience, humility and continuous improvement. This is simply how you become stronger. It isn’t about having more power.

You might spend several conversations on the same difficulty. Each one gives you something new to think about, but you keep returning to the same problem without doing anything differently. There is nothing wrong with taking time to understand a situation. Sometimes you need to talk things through before you are ready to act. But if the conversations continue without a clearer direction, it is worth asking what you are hoping to get from them.

- Are you understanding the problem better?
- Are you considering options you hadn’t thought about before?
- Are you making decisions, testing new approaches, or learning something about how you respond to challenges?
- Or are you simply talking about the same things without getting anywhere?

These aren’t always easy questions, especially when you have invested time, trust and sometimes money. Still, you should be able to raise them. You shouldn’t have to pretend things are working just because you keep showing up.

## What does progress actually look like?

Progress doesn’t always mean achieving a major goal or completely changing your life. Sometimes it simply means being able to be happy with what you have. Sometimes it is much less dramatic than you expect.

You might notice that a decision which once seemed impossible is becoming easier. Perhaps you begin to recognize a familiar pattern before it takes over, or you finally take action on something you have been putting off for months. The changes depend on what you are working on, but here are a few things worth paying attention to.

### You are becoming clearer about what you want

You might start feeling confused about your next step. Over time, you may develop a better understanding of what is important to you, what you want to change, and what you are willing to do about it. You may not have every answer, but you have a clearer direction than you did before.

### You are making decisions instead of endlessly going over them

Sometimes the difficulty isn’t a lack of options. It is being unable to choose between them. Progress might mean recognizing what is holding you back, deciding what matters most, and accepting that no decision comes with complete certainty. You may still feel nervous about your choice. The difference is that you are no longer waiting to feel perfectly ready before moving forward.

Sometimes having too many options isn’t really a good thing, because it makes things complicated. When I was a kid and bought an ice cream, there were only two options: vanilla or chocolate. Now there is a ton. The way I see it, a lack of simplicity leads to bad things.

### You are doing something differently

Understanding yourself is useful, but it is easy to spend a lot of time analyzing your behavior without changing it. Look for practical examples. Have you had a conversation you have been avoiding? Taken a step toward a goal? Responded differently to a situation that usually frustrates you? The action doesn’t have to be huge. What matters is whether something is beginning to change outside the conversation. And being different isn’t a bad thing.

### You are learning to handle things on your own

This is an important one. Are you becoming more comfortable thinking through your own problems? Can you recognize when you are falling into an old pattern? Do you find it easier to decide what to do without someone else telling you? You may still value your guide’s perspective, but you shouldn’t feel that you need them present every time you face a difficult decision. The ability to guide yourself is part of what makes progress meaningful.

There is a Zen idea worth remembering here, called *shoshin*, or beginner’s mind: to approach things with openness even when you already know a lot. In his book *Zen Mind, Beginner’s Mind*, Shunryu Suzuki wrote that in the beginner’s mind there are many possibilities, but in the expert’s mind there are few. Curiosity is what keeps a person growing.

## Four questions to ask yourself

You don’t need a complicated system to assess your progress. You just need to be willing to look honestly at what is happening.

**1. How do I react when I am asked a simple question?**

To me, it is easier to explain with an example. If someone asks you why you were late, and you answer “why are you so offensive all the time?”, that is being too defensive, and it is ego talking. Being too vulnerable can be ego as well. Notice how you react. Sometimes the person only asked a question.

**2. What is different now?**

What drives you now compared with when you started? Is there a different attitude in how you approach tomorrow?

**3. What am I still struggling with?**

Name it. Stress, ego, hurry, worry, fear, excuses, pride, overthinking, people-pleasing: each takes something from you. When we simplify our minds and stop managing everyone else’s emotions, we create space for a bigger, more fulfilling life.

**4. Do I understand what we are working toward?**

Often we are pulled to believe common nonsense phrases. You can move a mountain, and you can break rocks, but those are different things. Be clear about which one you are actually trying to do. If you can’t name the goal, say so.

## What if you are talking but nothing changes?

Then say it out loud. Ask what is missing, whether the goal needs to be clearer, or whether the approach needs to change. Don’t put the blame on yourself or on the other person. Sometimes the conversation simply isn’t the right tool, and sometimes you need a pause, or a different kind of support, such as a licensed professional if what you are dealing with feels heavy.

## How long should you wait to see results?

There is no accurate answer. Don’t think about the time issue, or you will be distracted. A long journey is ahead of everyone in this life, so my advice is to be patient and enjoy the process. Rely on yourself, and find the courage to stand on your own, because a lot of strength is waiting inside you to be used.

## How I approach progress

What comes to mind when you hear the words inner peace? For me, it begins with the understanding that your beginning does not decide your ending. Where you started doesn’t have to define where you arrive. Hard beginnings can become a source of strength. Ask a friend whose story started hard and turned out beautiful.

## When is it time to reconsider?

When you are staying in your comfort zone and nothing challenges you. When you keep avoiding finishing what you started. When the conversations stopped teaching you anything. If it doesn’t challenge you, it is unlikely to change you. And if it no longer serves a useful purpose, it is okay to reconsider.

## So, does it work?

Guidance can be useful, but its value isn’t measured by how many conversations you have or how inspiring one feels. Look at what you are learning, the decisions you are making, the actions you are taking, and whether you are becoming better able to handle challenges in your own life.

If you are not seeing progress, talk about it. Revisit your goals, ask questions, and consider whether the approach needs to change. And if it no longer serves a useful purpose, it is okay to step away.

Good guidance should leave you better able to guide yourself afterward. The goal isn’t simply to have more conversations. It is to make those conversations useful in the life you are living outside them.
