Looking for a Tony Robbins Coaching Alternative? What to Compare Before You Buy

If you have been looking into personal development or life coaching, there is a good chance you have come across Tony Robbins.

He is one of the most recognizable names in the personal development world, with books, events, courses, programs, and coaching.

But what if his approach isn't exactly what you are looking for?

Maybe the price doesn't work for you. Maybe you prefer a quieter coaching style. Maybe you don't want a large program or event. Or maybe you simply want to speak with one person who can get to know you and your situation over time.

Looking for an alternative doesn't necessarily mean that something is wrong with Tony Robbins.

It may simply mean that you are looking for something different.

Coaching style matters more than the name

Two life coaches can have completely different approaches.

One might be energetic and direct.

Another might spend most of the session asking questions and helping you reach your own conclusions.

One coach might give you exercises between sessions.

Another might focus almost entirely on conversation.

Neither approach automatically works for everyone.

This is something people sometimes overlook when choosing a coach.

They look at the coach's reputation, qualifications, social media following, or testimonials, but don't spend enough time thinking about whether they actually like the way that person communicates.

You are going to be having personal conversations with this person.

What should you look for in a private coach?

If you decide that you want one-to-one coaching, I would pay attention to a few things.

Second, do they understand what you are actually trying to accomplish?

Third, are they clear about how they work?

Fourth, do you feel comfortable talking honestly with them?

And finally, do you feel that they are interested in helping you become more capable, rather than simply keeping you dependent on coaching?

Questions to ask before choosing a coach

Before you pay for a coaching program or commit to a long-term relationship, consider asking:

What exactly are we going to work on?

How would you describe your coaching style?

How much structure will there be?

What do you expect from me?

What can I expect from you?

How often will we meet?

Can I speak with you before I commit?

What happens if I decide the coaching isn't working for me?

You don't have to know all the answers before your first conversation.

But asking these questions can help you avoid choosing a coach simply because they are famous. Fame and money can change people the negative way.
